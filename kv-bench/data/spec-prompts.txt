The recommendation to keep the key-value cache in f16 rests on a measurement that
is easy to misread. On a hybrid architecture, most layers hold no cache at all,
so a quantisation that appears to halve cache memory in the papers halves only a
small slice of the working set here. Worse, the sparse attention scheme bounds
how much of that cache any single decoding step touches, which means the bytes
saved were never on the critical path. The measured throughput delta lands inside
the noise of the harness, while the change in per-token divergence does not: it is
small but statistically real, and it is concentrated in exactly the positions where
the model is uncertain, which is where a decoding change is most visible to a user.
The conclusion is not that quantised caches are useless, but that they are a memory
instrument, not a speed instrument, and must be justified by capacity rather than
by latency.
